# Code of conduct

## The short version

This repository actively invites people to tell its author he is wrong. That
only works if being wrong is cheap and saying so is safe. Both halves matter:
an expert who cannot correct a chapter without a fight will stop bothering, and
a beginner who gets mocked for a naive question will never ask a second one —
and the naive questions are the ones this guide most needs.

So the standard here is narrow and easy to hold:

**Attack the claim. Never the person.** "Chapter 08 is wrong and here is the
`tcpdump`" is exactly right, however bluntly you put it. "You clearly have no
idea what you are doing" is not, however correct you turn out to be.

**Nobody is too new to be here.** There is no such thing as too obvious a
question. If someone asks one, answer it or leave it alone.

**Confidence carries a receipt.** Say what you measured, on what version, when.
An unsupported assertion delivered with certainty is the failure mode this whole
project is built to route around.

**Assume the other person is trying.** Most disagreements here are two people
with different stacks, different versions, or different threat models. Find out
which before deciding they are careless.

## Not acceptable

Harassment, sustained personal attacks, or demeaning comments. Sexualised
language or imagery. Insults about someone's identity — background, gender,
nationality, disability, religion, experience level, or anything else about who
they are rather than what they said. Publishing someone's private information.
Deliberately derailing threads. Continuing to press someone after they have
asked you to stop.

## Scope

Issues, pull requests, discussions, commit messages, and any space where someone
is representing this project.

## If something goes wrong

Report it privately through
[GitHub's private reporting form](https://github.com/aenawi/secure-remote-access/security/advisories/new)
— it is the security advisory form, but it is the private channel this
repository has, and it works for this too. Say what happened and link the thread.

Reports are read by the maintainer alone and are not shared further. You will
get a reply. If the report is about the maintainer, say so plainly and it will
still be answered — that is uncomfortable and it is also the only way a
one-person project's code of conduct means anything.

Responses run from a private word, through editing or removing comments, to a
temporary or permanent ban. Which one depends on severity and on what happens
next, not on how useful the person's contributions have been.
